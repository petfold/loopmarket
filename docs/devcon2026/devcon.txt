Brief outline (can be modified):

Slide 1

Motivation:
abstract principle:  we want better voluntary human cooperation between strangers, i.e. free markets (essential part of individual freedoms)
practical problems: existing internet commerce is highly inefficient (search, browsing multiple websites, following conditions on each, and then hoping that what we find is good enough. We can never be sure we found the best (or even nearly best) deal. Amazon, eBay (centralised, take their fees and can censor) or your own webshop (if you want to sell) or searching for and browsing many individual retailers' sites. Huge search and discoverability problem. Most potential trades/interactions don't happen.

Solution:
a new kind of market: universal, combinatorial, and permissionless

universal : any products and services, not just tokens (going beyond DEXes). Especially those that are possible to define formally.
combinatorial: multiple offers are combined automatically to satisfy a want, let the algorithms do the searching
permissionless: offers are registered on a permissionless, distributed, peer-to-peer "server", Swarm

not search and browse, instead describe precisely what is offered as a give or a want, and loopmarket finds a solution

1 slide about Swarm:

The real web3

Decentralized server
Infrastructure for data, applications, and communication
Host and publish without relying on a single provider or platform
Content identified by what it is, not where it lives (content-addressing and feeds)
Access control & client side encryption and access control
Prepay for storage (rent-a-storage, MB*month); no per-download charge to the publisher
Values built into the protocol, trustless and permissionless network
100% of protocol storage payments (BZZ) go to node operators
network self-sufficient and resilient network
storage prices adjust to supply and demand automatically with network utilization.
Publishing without a hosting account.
Content is distributed across thousands of independent nodes, so its availability does not depend on a single provider.

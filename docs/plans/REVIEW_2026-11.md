and the fixes after it), done overnight on Peter's request ("a full code
review of loopmarket and its documentation"). Discussion draft: the
recommendations are proposals, not decisions. Seven plain defects found

## 1. Summary

The exact arithmetic, the canonical records, and the clearing checklist
are carried through with care, and each reviewer's report has a list of
what it checked and found sound. The weak side is the one the federated
book opened in October: **what a stranger's announced book can do to
everyone who folds it.** Before tonight one announced book could

- hide other makers' items and holds from every reader (R3-1),
- stop every command-line reader's fold (R3-3),
- and a maker could not see the loop that cleared its own offers (R3-6).

Those are fixed (§2). Still open in the same family: a record with a
wrong-typed field (R1-1), an announcement that is not UTF-8 (BR-5), two
rival clearing books under a chain (R5b-2), a book of a few kilobytes
that reads as millions of records (R3-5, question 55), and a clearing
book dated into the past (R3-2, question 54).

The second theme is **the command line under the default catalogue**.
The tests and the documents' examples run on a hand-made `market.od`.
A person who ran `loop setup` has ontodag's `core`, where `home`,
`option` and `5g` are everyday categories. There the README's own
`loop place home …` silently makes offers with no place (R5a F1,
question 58) and `loop option` publishes vocabulary nobody can want
(R5a F3).

The third is **documents that lag**: ROADMAP's P1 section, the guide's
`registry 5.0`, REFERENCE's ontodag floor (§4.5).

## 3. Questions for Peter

Numbered on from ontodag's list (54 to 62).

### 54. What is a clearing book's loop judged against?

The fold re-checks a clearing book's loop at the record's own
`found_at` and does not ask whether an offer was withdrawn
(`BookClearing.recheck`). `BookClearing.submit` refuses a withdrawn
offer and reads the live clock. So the re-check is weaker than the
clearing it repeats (R3-2, R2-05).

*Example.* Three makers publish a triangle and then all withdraw; no
clearing ever happened. Mallory clears the triangle on a private copy
of the old roots, dates it to when the offers stood, and announces that
book. Every reader's fold marks the six withdrawn offers filled.

*Options.*

1. Leave it, and write it into THREATS: without a chain a loop's date
   is the clearing's word. No work; the makers stay exposed to a false
   "filled".
2. A loop counts from the moment the chain saw it: a clearing book's
   root is anchored (a beat, or an announcement of the root), and a
   loop anchored after a withdrawal the reader sees is rejected. Every
   reader still computes one fold. Needs the anchor, which today's
   announcement (a book, not a root) does not give.
3. Each reader remembers what it has seen and rejects a loop first seen
   after the withdrawal. No chain needed; two readers no longer compute
   the same fold.

*Recommendation.* 2, and 1 until it is built. Worth checking first how
much of this the finalized beats already settle when `beat` is set.

### 55. Is there a bound on an announced book's size?

recordstore's trie nodes do not commit to their position, so one small
node can be the child of every branch above it (R3-5).

*Example.* 34 blobs, under 5 kB, announce 65,536 records: 3.2 s and
301 MB to fold here. Each further level multiplies by 16. THREATS T2
says a flooder pays per offer; this one pays for a handful of blobs.

*Options.*

1. The fold counts while it reads and rejects a book whole past a cap
   (say 10,000 records), with a rejection line. Simple and bounded; the
   cap is a protocol number every folder must share, and a large honest
   maker meets it.
2. recordstore refuses a node that appears at two positions. The right
   depth for the fix; a format change there.
3. Both: 1 now, 2 raised with recordstore.

*Recommendation.* 3.

### 56. Does a loop of divisible legs need the per-node check?

The per-node check (`per_node_ok`) is skipped when every leg is
divisible, on the ground that quantities scale. Since 2026-09-14 fills
take the wants' own quantities and never scale (R2-03, R5a X1).

*Example.* amara: `give 10kg apple 100`, `want 10kg vegetable-box 45`.
bruno: `give 10kg vegetable-box 40`, `want 10kg apple 110`. The loop
clears 10 kg each way: amara hands over what she priced at 100 for
what she priced at 45.

*Options.*

1. Run the per-node check on every loop. No maker gives more than it
   gets on its own scale; fewer loops clear.
2. Scale the quantities, as ARCHITECTURE §7 and P2-clearing-pricing §6
   say. That is P2's pricing work.
3. Keep the code and correct the two documents: a price is a rate per
   leg, not a floor on what a maker gets back.

*Recommendation.* 1 now, 2 when P2's pricing is built.

### 57. Is an offer's catalogue pin compared with the checking catalogue?

Two offers that pin root X are matched and cleared by `BookClearing`
under a catalogue whose root is Y; only the two offers' pins are
compared with each other (R1-7, R5b-5). The contract's verifier is read
as requiring the beat's root on every offer, so the book path is the
weaker one. The October review decided this for the majors and left the
root open.

*Example.* Under X a `tutoring` is not a `lesson`; under Y it is. A
session on Y clears a tutoring give against a lesson want, both pinned
to X, where their makers' catalogue said no.

*Options.*

1. Clearing refuses an offer whose root is not the clearing's. Exact;
   every catalogue update strands the open offers until they are
   published again.
2. Check each offer under the root it pins, when the reader can open
   it. Exact and kind to old offers; costs opening other roots.
3. Keep it: a newer catalogue of the same major only adds knowledge.
   True for what ontodag guarantees of additions; a merge can add an
   edge the maker never saw.

*Recommendation.* 1 for clearing, with listing and matching left as
they are and a note on the offer.

### 58. A place named with a word the catalogue holds

`loop place NAME` accepts any name; on an offer line a catalogue name
wins before the personal layer is looked at (R5a F1).

*Example.* Under the default catalogue amara in Ljubljana and bruno in
Vienna each type what the README shows: `loop place home LAT,LON±5km
ADDRESS`, `loop set terms home`. Their offers carry the category
`home`, no rectangle and no handoff address, and they match, 280 km
apart.

*Options.*

1. `place` refuses a name the pinned catalogue holds; the documents
   use another name.
2. A name the person declared wins over the catalogue's. The documents
   work as written; the category `home` is out of that person's reach.
3. Catalogue first as now, with a note line when a name is both.

*Recommendation.* 1, and 3's note for a place declared before the
catalogue gained the word.

### 59. Which side is `bond` for?

`bond` and `deductible` land on wants as well as gives. REFERENCE §17,
the setting's help and the guide say "my deposit on every give", and
`loop deposit` funds gives only, so a want's declared deposit is never
funded from the command line (R5a F14). Matching does read a want's
deposit.

*Options.* Gives only, as documented. Or both, and then the three
documents change and `deposit` funds wants.

*Recommendation.* Gives only; a want's deposit, if wanted, under its
own setting so that it is asked for by name.

### 60. `export` then `import`

`export` writes every offer record and nothing else; `import` publishes
each. Withdrawn and filled offers come back open (R5a F6, D1 F16).

*Options.* Export open offers only (two lines). Or carry the
tombstones and signatures and restore them (moving a book, which is
what the guide says the pair is for).

*Recommendation.* The first now; the second when someone needs to move
a book with its history.

### 61. What a batch exits with

A batch exits with its last command's code, so failed lines in the
middle exit 0 (R5b-17).

*Options.* As now. Run every line and exit non-zero if any failed.
Stop at the first failure.

*Recommendation.* Run every line, exit with the first non-zero code.

### 62. A link that names where the page loads its code from

`?pyodide=` lets a link name the site the page loads its Python
runtime from, and `?gateway=` the gateway it reads through (BR-4).
ontodag's page has the same lines.

*Options.*

1. Drop `?pyodide=`: the runtime comes from the page's own copy or the
   pinned version, nothing else.
2. Keep it behind a question that names the site.
3. Keep as is.

*Recommendation.* 1. `?gateway=` can stay once the page checks feed
updates read over HTTP (BR-7; it already ships `signerOf`), since a
gateway then cannot pass off content.

  ontodag has `tests/test_reference.py` for its reference; loopmarket

  command line (R5b-2), or an `export` of a book holding a withdrawn
